speaker:  Ed Bueler, University of Alaska Fairbanks

title:  The mathematical (and numerical) problem of glaciation

abstract:  Which land is glaciated, and how that changes over time, depends on bed topography and on climate, through the surface mass balance.  It also depends on how ice flows under gravity, which is an expensive-to-compute function of the current ice geometry and basal conditions.  I will argue that it is useful to pose the problem abstractly: for any choice of stress balance and sliding law, how does the ice geometry evolve, and in particular how does the ice-covered area change?  In any complete model the location of the ice margin is an output, the solution of a free-boundary problem.  First I will review older results for the shallow ice approximation, including the observation that steady states on bumpy beds need not be unique.  Then I will turn to Stokes flow with a free surface, where there has been real progress in the last two years, including work by Josefin Ahlkrona's group.  In the Stokes model, ice provably flows downhill on average, and numerical evidence suggests that surface motion damps surface bumps.  These facts clarify the well-posedness of the model, and they justify stabilizations which allow much longer stable time steps for the coupled Stokes and free-surface model.  The theory also shows precisely where numerical errors in surface elevation come from.  These developments bring Stokes models closer to practical, scalable use in simulating glaciers and ice sheets.
